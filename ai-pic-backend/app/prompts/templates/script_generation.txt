## [Important] Dialogue content requirements
This script will need voice acting. Arrange dialogue appropriately based on scene type:

**Scene types and dialogue requirements**:
1. **Dialogue scenes** (character interaction, emotional expression): need rich dialogue, 3-5 exchanges or more
2. **Action scenes** (chases, fights, operations): may have brief dialogue or no dialogue
3. **Atmosphere scenes** (environment display, mood rendering): usually do not need dialogue; use stage directions instead
4. **Transition scenes** (scene changes, passage of time): generally do not need dialogue

**Overall dialogue volume**:
- Each minute of dialogue usually needs about 100-150 Chinese characters at a normal speaking rate
- A {{ episode.duration_minutes or 30 }}-minute script needs about {{ ((episode.duration_minutes or 30) * 120)|int }}-{{ ((episode.duration_minutes or 30) * 150)|int }} Chinese characters of total dialogue
- Dialogue can be distributed flexibly across scenes: more in key scenes, none in atmosphere scenes if appropriate

**Dialogue quality requirements**:
- Dialogue should feel natural and fluent, matching each character's personality
- Include emotional expression, reactions, follow-up questions, etc.
- Avoid using "..." to skip dialogue; write the full line
- Dialogue format: Character name: 'line of dialogue' or Character name: "line of dialogue"
 - **Knowledge gating**: Characters must not reference information before obtaining it; if a name or reveal appears for the first time, use dialogue/action to explain *how it was learned* (self-introduction / someone else's introduction / badge / phone note / eyewitness / inference, etc.), or use a vague form of address until the reveal is justified

## Script structure guidance
### Opening (first 10%)
- Establish the setting and atmosphere
- Introduce the main characters' current state
- Introduce the episode's core problem

### Development (middle 70%)
- Advance the plot step by step
- Unfold character conflicts
- Build suspense and tension

### Climax (last 15%)
- Bring the conflict to its peak
- Present the key moment of choice
- Deliver the emotional explosion point

### Ending (final 5%)
- Resolve the problem or trigger a turn
- Plant foreshadowing for the next episode
- Land the emotional beat
